Then evaluate it across these areas:

## Front matter
- `title` — clear, specific, not clickbait
- `description` — one punchy sentence that hooks the reader; matches the post
- `intro` — sets up the argument well; ends with a reason to keep reading
- `tags` and `categories` — appropriate and consistent with the site
- `tldr` — if present, is it actually a useful summary?

## Structure & flow
- Does the opening land immediately, or does it warm up too slowly?
- Are headers meaningful section breaks, or just labels?
- Is the argument or narrative logically sequenced?
- Does it end with a clear point, takeaway, or call to action?

## Voice & tone
- Personal but not self-indulgent — Jack writes from experience, not ego
- Data-informed where relevant, not jargon-heavy
- Honest and direct; avoids corporate hedging

## Content quality
- Are claims backed up (references, links, or personal evidence)?
- Are there any gaps in the argument?
- Anything that could be cut without losing anything?

## Mechanics
- Obvious typos or grammar issues
- Links present where useful (hashtags, references)
- Footnotes `[^n]` used correctly if present

## Output format
Give a short verdict (1–2 sentences), then flag specific issues with line references where possible. End with the single most important thing to fix.
